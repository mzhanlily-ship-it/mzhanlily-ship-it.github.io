Supply chain · Academic team project

Designing a regional distribution network

How many distribution centres should a seasonal ski-accessories business operate, and what does additional coverage buy?

I built the anyLogistix model using data collected by teammates. After the team validated the model, I carried out the network comparison analysis. The results below are team-report outputs.

The decision and the model

We compared an Auckland-only network with two centres (Auckland and Christchurch) and three centres (adding Wellington). The one-year simulation considered base, higher-demand and lower-demand conditions, followed by an Auckland disruption scenario.

What changed the recommendation?

+6.4 ppBase service: 3 DC versus 2 DC
+1.9%Base modelled profit: 3 DC versus 2 DC
87.3%3 DC service under high demand

The third centre improves both reported profit and service in every matched demand scenario. However, its base profit advantage over two centres is modest. The high-demand case still misses some demand even with three centres: greater revenue is not the same as adequate service.

The separate disruption discussion reports approximately NZD 2.98 million profit for three centres versus NZD 2.73 million for two. This supports the resilience argument, but these approximate narrative figures are not pooled with the table above.

Disruption evidence: report §§5.4 and 7. These are simulated outcomes, not realised business performance.

Recommendation and limits

Use three centres as the preferred modelled configuration, then test whether the additional facility is justified under current costs and a defined service target. Two centres remain a useful comparison because the incremental base profit gain is small.

The recommendation depends on demand, replenishment and cost assumptions. The published report has inconsistent figures across sections and does not provide a complete reproducible output history. This page shows the selected comparison set transparently rather than treating it as a verified forecast.
